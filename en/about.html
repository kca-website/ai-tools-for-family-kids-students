About us / FAQ

An independent educational project created to make AI more useful, understandable and responsible in everyday learning.

In one sentence: aitools4kids.gr is an independent, free Greek guide that helps parents, students and educators find which AI tool suits each subject and need, without turning AI into a machine for ready-made answers.

Who we are

aitools4kids.gr was built with care and a lot of personal work, with one simple aim: to offer free, practical help to parents, students and educators who want to use Artificial Intelligence as a learning aid, not as a substitute for thinking.

Frequently asked questions

Why was it created?

To provide a free, Greek point of reference that connects a school need with the right tool and a pedagogically useful way to use it. The project is learning-first: understanding and effort come first, AI help comes after.

How is it funded?

Access to aitools4kids.gr is free and the site does not rely on advertising or affiliate commissions from tools. If the funding model ever changes, it will be stated clearly so that the independence of the recommendations stays visible.

Which AI does it use and where does the data go?

The main server-side AI Help is routed first through Cloudflare Workers AI and, if needed, through Groq, using GPT-OSS 120B. Puter is an optional alternative. The text you submit is sent to the active provider to generate an answer; aitools4kids.gr does not store the conversation in its own database. The current provider order is shown on the AI Transparency page.

AI Transparency · Privacy Policy (in Greek)

Why don't primary and lower-secondary (Gymnasio) students get a direct tutor?

The site's direct student AI Help is available only for upper-secondary school (Lykeio). For primary school and Gymnasio, the Parent/Educator Assistant is used instead, so that AI support goes through an adult and does not encourage younger children to use a chatbot directly.

How are tools selected and checked?

We prioritise official provider terms and documentation, official educational sources and the Greek school framework. Where documentation is insufficient, we say so instead of filling gaps with assumptions.
