AI tools for students: what is worth using and how

Artificial intelligence can help a student understand a concept better, organise their studying or check an attempt. But not every tool suits every age, and it is not a good idea to use it to hand in ready-made answers.

Short answer

aitools4kids.gr is a free educational platform for students, parents and educators. It connects the Greek school curriculum with AI Study, a Practice Map and suitable AI tools by age and subject.

Quick tips:
  • Choose age and subject first, not “the most famous AI”.
  • Use AI for explanations, questions and checking, not for copying.
  • Check the age and privacy terms of every service.
  • If you don't know where to start, use the 2-minute Practice Map.

Which AI tool suits a student?

There is no single right answer for everyone. A student who struggles with reading comprehension needs a different tool from a student who wants to check a maths procedure, organise sources or practise a foreign language.

That is why, on AI Tools for Kids, recommendations are grouped by age zone and role. A student or parent can see what is suitable for Primary, Gymnasio (lower secondary) or Lykeio (upper secondary), instead of searching through one single catalogue.

Three uses with real educational value

1. Explaining something in a different way

A student can ask for a simpler explanation, an example or a comprehension question. Good use is not “give me the answer” but “help me understand the next step”.

2. Checking your attempt

After you solve an exercise or write a text, a tool can point out unclear parts, ask why you chose a method or show you what is worth looking at again.

3. Organising your studying

AI can turn a long chapter into small steps, create revision questions or help build a study plan. The final understanding, however, must stay with the student.

The site also has a Practice Map.

In a few minutes it shows which areas need more practice and links the result to suitable tools and Learning Paths.

What changes with age

Primary: priority on adult supervision, short interactions and tools that do not encourage unsupervised chatting. See the dedicated guide AI for primary school children (in Greek).

Gymnasio (lower secondary): more independent use begins, but with emphasis on sources, checking answers and age limits. See AI for Gymnasio students (in Greek).

Lykeio (upper secondary): AI becomes more useful for research, building arguments and complex problems, together with a greater need for fact-checking and proper citation of sources.

What a student should avoid

  • Handing in text or a solution they do not understand.
  • Uploading personal or sensitive data without reason.
  • Assuming every AI answer is correct.
  • Getting around a service's age requirements.
  • Using AI instead of making their own first attempt.

For students who need accessible material

The site also has a collection of 167 school concepts in Greek Sign Language (GSL), organised by subject and class, with a simple explanation and direct links to official videos from the Institute of Educational Policy (IEP).
