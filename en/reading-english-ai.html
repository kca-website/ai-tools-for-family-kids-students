Reading and language

Do you want to practise reading, pronunciation or English?

Focused tools can be more useful than a general chatbot here: they can listen to reading, identify difficult words or organise short language practice around a clear goal.

ReadingPronunciationEnglishSupervision where needed
Quick choice:
  • Microsoft Reading Coach: for reading aloud and targeted practice on difficult words.
  • Read Along by Google: a free Android reading app for children who already know the basics of the alphabet in a supported language.
  • Duolingo: for broader language practice and daily revision.

Microsoft Reading Coach — reading aloud with feedback

Reading Coach is free and uses AI and reading-fluency detection to identify words that cause difficulty and suggest targeted practice. It can also create age-appropriate stories for reading practice.

What to know

A Microsoft account is required and Microsoft recommends parent or educator supervision for generative-AI features. Its usefulness also depends on the language you want to practise.

Read Along by Google — a free Android option

Read Along listens while a child reads aloud, offers help when needed and gives small rewards. After the initial download it can work offline.

Where it fits

It is mainly useful for younger readers who already have basic alphabet knowledge in the language they are practising. Check current language support before recommending it for a specific language.

Duolingo — daily language practice

If the goal is vocabulary, short exercises and daily repetition, a dedicated language-learning tool can be clearer than open-ended conversation with a general AI assistant.

See the Duolingo card →

Use it well

  • Keep sessions short and repeat the words that are genuinely difficult.
  • Do not rely only on an app score; try reading or speaking without help.
  • For younger children, a parent should review account, microphone and privacy settings.
